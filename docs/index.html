Nāda (नाद) is Sanskrit for "sound": in Indian music theory, the primordial sound every note comes from.

nāda is a music player for people who live on the keyboard. Songs on your disk and music from YouTube share one queue. Play a song and, like YouTube, nāda lines up what comes next; reorder or remove anything with vim keys. Lyrics scroll along with the song. If you like a YouTube song, D saves it to your music folder, with its tags, cover and lyrics, while it keeps playing. It runs on MPD, so your library, queue and playback carry on with the window closed.

#Features

  • Search everything from one key. / opens a floating search over whatever you're on: your library answers as you type, YouTube a moment later, with the best match on top; Tab picks where to look (YouTube, library, playlists, commands, the list you're on). Play, play next, add or download without leaving the box.
  • Now is the lyrics. The window opens on the playing song's lyrics, large, the current line lit and centred, scrolling with the song. Click a line to jump there. On the right: the cover, the song's real name and artists (from YouTube Music, not the upload's title), the album, views, and what's next.
  • Lyrics that show up. Looked up in YouTube Music first (by the video itself: synced lyrics licensed from LyricFind), then LRCLIB and NetEase; a .lrc next to the file or lyrics in its tags come first. A source that can't be reached is asked again in 30 s, never remembered as "no lyrics". Hindi lyrics show in Latin letters (Hinglish) or Devanagari. L opens the lyrics panel: every version the sources have, which one fits the song's length, [ ] to fix the timing, s for the script.
  • Up next, like YouTube. Playing a YouTube song lines up YouTube's own mix for it; a song from your library brings the rest of its album and more by that artist. The queue shows what you added and what autoplay added separately, refills itself as it runs low, and M turns it off.
  • Your queue, edited like text. dd removes, J/K move a song, yy/p copy and paste, V selects several, u undoes, Ctrl+r redoes, and you can drag rows by their handle or click × to remove one.
  • Seek anywhere. , and . skip 10 seconds (3. skips 30), the ⟲ ⟳ buttons do the same, and the progress bar is clickable.
  • YouTube without an account. Search it from /; every result shows its views and age, and "All YouTube results" opens the full list. Stream links expire after a few hours, so nāda remembers the video, not the link, and renews it before it plays.
  • Download what you like. D saves a YouTube song as Opus (what YouTube streams, so nothing is re-encoded) into ~/Music/YouTube/<Artist>/<Title>.opus, with tags, cover art and a .lrc lyrics file. When it finishes, the queue entry is swapped for the file in place, and playback carries on from the same second.
  • Your library in columns, like yazi: artists, albums, genres or folders (Tab), h/l between columns, / searches everything.
  • Playlists that mix files and YouTube songs: :w name saves the queue, :e name loads one.
  • A mini player (z) in the corner, with the current lyric line.
  • Everywhere on the desktop: MPRIS for media keys, playerctl and your phone (KDE Connect); a waybar module; nada ctl for scripts and key bindings; a notification with the cover when the song changes.
  • Never freezes: anything slow (YouTube, lyrics, covers) runs in the background.

#Install

#Requirements

  • Python 3.11+, PyGObject, GTK 4
  • MPD (the engine: it plays, keeps the queue and indexes your music)
  • yt-dlp for YouTube, and ffmpeg for downloads
  • python-ytmusicapi for lyrics from YouTube Music and songs' real names (without it, LRCLIB and NetEase only)
  • optional: gtk4-layer-shell (the mini player floats in a corner), mutagen (tags on downloads), a Nerd Font for the icons
# Arch
sudo pacman -S --needed python-gobject gtk4 mpd yt-dlp ffmpeg python-ytmusicapi python-mutagen gtk4-layer-shell ttf-jetbrains-mono-nerd
systemctl --user enable --now mpd

MPD needs a music_directory and an audio output; the Arch wiki has a five-line config. nāda finds MPD the way every MPD client does: MPD_HOST / MPD_PORT, else MPD's socket in $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/mpd/socket, else localhost:6600.

#nāda

git clone https://github.com/Theyashsawarkar/nada ~/.local/share/nada
ln -s ~/.local/share/nada/bin/nada ~/.local/bin/nada
nada            # opens the player (it starts itself the first time)

Update with git -C ~/.local/share/nada pull && nada quit.

#Usage

nada                       open the player, or close it if it's open
nada show | hide | mini    the full player, hidden, the mini player
nada tab <name>            open on a tab: home queue library youtube playlists downloads
nada add [--next|--play] <YouTube URL | file | search words>
nada ctl toggle|play|pause|stop|next|prev
nada ctl seek +10 | seek -10 | seek 1:30
nada ctl vol +5 | vol -5 | vol 40
nada status [--json]       what's playing
nada waybar                JSON for a waybar module
nada daemon | quit         start / stop the player (MPD keeps playing)

The player is one resident process; nada only sends it a D-Bus message, so it opens at once. ctl, status and waybar talk to MPD directly and work even when the player isn't running. Closing the window (q, Esc) only hides it: the music, autoplay and downloads carry on.

#Commands

Typed after :, like vim.

CommandDoes
:w <name>Save the queue as a playlist (YouTube songs included)
:e <name>Replace the queue with a playlist (:e alone opens Playlists)
:yt <search>Play the top YouTube result, and its mix after it
:play <search>Play a song from your library (falls back to YouTube)
:add <url or search>Add to the end of the queue
:seek 1:30 · :seek +30Jump to a time, or by an amount
:vol 40 · :vol +10Set or change the volume
:sleep 30m · :sleep offFade out and pause after a while
:lyrics <search>Pick the lyrics by hand (remembered for the song)
:autoplay on · offSame as M
:dlDownload the playing song
:clearEmpty the queue (u brings it back)
:q · :qaHide the window · quit the player

#Configuration

Everything is optional, in ~/.config/nada/config.toml:

[nada]
autoplay = true          # line up more songs after yours (M toggles it, and it's remembered)
download_dir = "YouTube" # inside MPD's music directory
format = "opus"          # opus (no re-encoding) | mp3 | m4a
seek_step = 10           # seconds for , and .
volume_step = 5
notify = true            # a notification when the song changes (while the window is hidden)
opacity = 0.62           # window background: 1 is solid, lower shows the blur behind it
                         # (unset: your varna theme's panel glass, so it matches Vyom's panels)
music_dir = ""           # default: MPD's music_directory

~/.config/nada/style.css is loaded last, to restyle anything (the default look is Catppuccin Mocha with a mauve accent: see nada/style.css). With varna installed, nāda takes the current theme's colours instead (varna tool nada <theme> gives it its own) and restyles as soon as the theme changes.

#Desktop integration

sway (the same works with any compositor's key bindings):

exec nada daemon
bindsym $mod+Shift+m exec nada                       # open / hide
bindsym $mod+Mod1+space exec nada ctl toggle          # without opening anything
bindsym $mod+Mod1+period exec nada ctl next
bindsym $mod+Mod1+comma exec nada ctl prev
for_window [app_id="^dev\.vayu\.Nada$"] floating enable, resize set 1180 720, move position center

Media keys going through playerctl work as they are: nāda speaks MPRIS. On SwayFX, blur enable, corner_radius 20 in that for_window rule gives the window its glass look; the mini player is a layer-shell surface named nada (layer_effects "nada" { blur enable; corner_radius 18; }).

waybar, a module that shows what's playing (and disappears when nothing is):

"custom/nada": {
  "exec": "nada waybar",
  "return-type": "json",
  "on-click": "nada ctl toggle",
  "on-click-middle": "nada ctl next",
  "on-click-right": "nada",
  "on-scroll-up": "nada ctl seek +10",
  "on-scroll-down": "nada ctl seek -10"
}

Its class is play, pause or stopped, to style each state.

From other tools: nada add --play <url> plays a YouTube link with its mix after it; raga, the YouTube search panel, uses exactly that.

#How it works

 raga, keys, waybar ──► nada (bin/nada: D-Bus) ──► the player process ──► MPD ──► speakers
                                                    │
                                                    ├─ yt-dlp: search, mixes, stream links, downloads
                                                    ├─ LRCLIB: lyrics
                                                    └─ MPRIS: media keys, phone, playerctl
  • nada/core.py is the model: MPD's queue and state, plus what MPD doesn't do (YouTube songs and their expiring links, autoplay, undo, downloads, lyrics, covers). Slow work runs on worker threads and reports back on the GTK main loop.
  • nada/window.py is the window and the vim key engine (modes, counts, pending operators, registers); nada/views.py the six tabs and the right pane; nada/mini.py the mini player; nada/mpris.py MPRIS.
  • YouTube songs sit in MPD's queue as stream URLs, tagged with their video id, so other MPD clients see and play them too.

#Troubleshooting

  • "MPD isn't running". systemctl --user start mpd (and enable it). nāda reconnects by itself.
  • No sound but it says it's playing. That's MPD's audio output: check audio_output in your mpd.conf (PipeWire users: type "pipewire" or "pulse").
  • "Volume control unavailable". Your MPD output has no mixer; set mixer_type "software" in its audio_output block.
  • A YouTube song won't play. Update yt-dlp first (yt-dlp -U, or your package manager): YouTube changes often and yt-dlp follows.
  • Wrong or missing lyrics. :lyrics <artist> <song> picks them by hand.
  • Anything else. The player's log has the details: journalctl --user -u nada -u nada-daemon -b (without systemd: ~/.local/state/nada/daemon.log); please open an issue with it.
